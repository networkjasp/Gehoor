Hearing care for babies and children

Good hearing is the foundation of speech, language and learning. We test children of every age, from their first days, gently and with lots of patience.

Newborn hearing screening

We screen newborns at Life Eugene Marais Hospital, right across the road from our Les Marais practice. The screen takes a few minutes while your baby sleeps, and it doesn't hurt.

The 1-3-6 guideline

  1. 1
    By 1 month: hearing screened.
  2. 3
    By 3 months: a full diagnostic test if the screen needs follow-up.
  3. 6
    By 6 months: support in place, such as hearing aids, if needed.

Hearing milestones to watch for

If your child isn't doing these things, or you're simply worried, trust your instinct and have their hearing checked.

AgeMost children can…
Birth – 3 monthsStartles at sudden loud sounds and calms to your voice.
4 – 6 monthsTurns eyes or head towards sounds and enjoys rattles.
7 – 12 monthsResponds to their name, babbles, and understands "no" and "bye-bye".
1 – 2 yearsPoints to familiar objects when named, and uses single words that grow into short phrases.
2 – 4 yearsSpeaks in sentences, is understood by family, and hears you from another room.
School ageFollows instructions in class, keeps up with reading, and doesn't turn the TV up loud.

Questions from parents

My baby "referred" on the newborn screen. Does that mean they are deaf?

No. A "refer" result only means the test needs to be repeated. It often happens because of fluid in the ears or a restless baby. Most babies pass the follow-up test, but it's important to do it promptly.

How do you test a baby who can't respond?

Newborn screening measures the ear's own response to soft sounds (otoacoustic emissions) while your baby sleeps. It's quick, painless and safe.

At what age can my child have a hearing test?

At any age, from birth. We adapt the test to your child's age and development, often using play.
